*"We negotiated a lower $/hr on our GPU contract."*

Cool. You're still overpaying.

Serious GPU work pushes you multi-cloud. Once you're across providers, **4 cost drivers compound underneath the hourly rate**. None of them are GPU pricing. Most teams don't track these.

## Data transfer between clouds costs $55K+/year

Data ingress is free. Egress costs **$0.08–$0.12/GB**.

An enterprise moving **50TB/month** between clouds pays **$55K+/year** in data transfer fees alone. Checkpoints, training data, model artifacts. Every hop between providers adds to the bill.

Nobody budgets for it until it's too late.

## Wrong GPU topology cuts training throughput 30–50%

**5ms of network latency cuts a 10 Gbps connection to 3.74 Gbps.**

Wrong GPU topology on multi-node distributed training causes **30–50% performance degradation**.

Most Kubernetes schedulers treat GPUs as opaque black boxes. No rack awareness. No zone optimization. Pods land wherever there's space, not where they'd run fastest.

## Average GPU utilization sits at 40%

Teams provision for peak demand. Average utilization sits at **40%**.

Fewer than **10% of companies hit 85%+ GPU utilization**.

So **30–50% of AI compute budgets** go to reserved hardware sitting cold. You're paying for GPUs not training anything.

## Same chip, 10x price spread across providers

**H100: $2.99/hr on one provider. $32.77/hr on another. Same chip. 10x spread.**

A100: **$1.89 vs $10.98**.

Single-cloud contracts mean you eat the spread.

---

Even teams going multi-cloud still eat costs 1 through 3 if they don't address them.

**The GPU hourly rate might be a vanity metric.**
